# v0.9.0 source trust-cap record

Status: unreleased product/schema candidate, validated on 2026-08-22. This record does not claim that a tag or release exists.

## Question

Can a malicious compact adapter that labels ordinary model metadata `verified` be prevented from obtaining verified authority unless the operator authorizes that exact source, while a trusted verifier remains verified and field selection stays independent of arrival order?

## Deterministic fixture

[`examples/source-trust-observations.json`](../../examples/source-trust-observations.json) supplies two model-metadata claims for one stable alias plus synthetic sensitive-content markers used only for leak assertions:

| Source | Self-reported level | Version | Expected effective level |
|---|---|---|---|
| `fixture-signature-verifier` | `verified` | `weights-v1` | `verified` because the exact source is authorized |
| `malicious-compact-adapter` | `verified` | `weights-v2` | `observed` because no rule matches |

[`examples/source-trust-policy.json`](../../examples/source-trust-policy.json) authorizes only `fixture-signature-verifier` for `verified` and independently caps `fixture-deployment-config` at `declared`. All unmatched sources have a fixed maximum of `observed`.

Reproduce the graph:

```bash
go build -o ./bin/aiebom ./cmd/aiebom

./bin/aiebom scan \
  --input examples/source-trust-observations.json \
  --source-trust-policy examples/source-trust-policy.json \
  --graph-out work/source-trust.evidence.json
```

The selected version and digest are `weights-v1` and `trusted-digest`. The competing `weights-v2` and `malicious-digest` candidates remain visible as conflicts with effective level `observed`; the malicious claim is not deleted or mislabeled as verified.

## Positive and negative controls

- With no policy file, a compact source claiming `verified` is capped to `observed`.
- Exact source matching is case-sensitive; a differently cased name does not inherit authority.
- A trusted exact-source verifier retains `verified`.
- A source rule can reduce observed input to `declared` or `inferred`.
- Trusted-first and malicious-first permutations produce deeply equal graph nodes. Evidence strength still precedes recency, and conflicts remain visible.
- The live receiver applies the same policy before metadata can enter its pending cross-batch correlation queue and counts unique reductions in `evidenceDowngrades`.
- Loading a persisted v0.8 graph reapplies current caps to node, edge, and field-candidate summaries and recomputes selected fields; an untrusted historical verified claim is reduced instead of silently surviving restart.
- Invalid policy versions, unknown fields, invalid levels, empty sources, duplicate exact-source rules, and trailing JSON are rejected.
- Synthetic input-message and tool-argument leak markers in the fixture do not reach the graph. Prompt, response, credential, tool result, description, and schema content are neither needed nor newly retained by the cap.

## Validation boundary

The policy is vendor-neutral and matches exact source labels rather than framework types or transport-specific fields. It solves source authorization, not source authentication: a compact document or OTLP resource can still self-report a source name. Operators may grant `verified` only when the adapter process or transport that can use that name is separately controlled or authenticated.

The graph's structural fields are unchanged from v0.8, but the shared product/schema version advances to `0.9.0` to record the new ingestion trust contract. Existing canonical profile v2 remains sufficient because no signed graph field semantics or ordering changed.
